Golden Analytics lands $14M seed extension and opens AI platform to public beta

Francois Ajenstat, Golden Analytics founder and CEO.
Golden Analytics
, the AI-native analytics startup founded by former Tableau product chief
Francois Ajenstat
, added $14 million to its seed financing round just two months after
emerging from stealth
, bringing its
total seed funding to $21 million
.
The additional funding,
announced Tuesday
, was led by Insight Partners, with existing backers NEA and Madrona re-upping. It coincides with the public beta launch of Golden’s
product
, which Ajenstat said has drawn early-access requests from about 1,000 companies, about one in six of them in the Fortune 500.
Golden Analytics is an AI-powered business intelligence tool. It connects to data stored in cloud warehouses or in uploaded files, analyzes it, and produces charts, dashboards and written summaries. Users can query the data in plain language or build visualizations themselves, and a control Ajenstat calls a “slider of autonomy” sets how much of the work the software does automatically.
Ajenstat said the addition of Insight alongside Madrona and NEA brings additional grounding in AI and analytics, and described the extension as validation of the company’s momentum.
Ganesh Bell
, a managing director at Insight, will take a seat on Golden’s board, Ajenstat said. Golden is also working with
George Mathew
, another Insight managing director and the former president of Alteryx.
The new capital will go toward engineering and go-to-market hires, Ajenstat said, with sales ramping up sooner than he had planned given the early demand. Golden has increased its team to seven people from four at April launch.
Its engineers are all based in the Seattle area, where said the company operates in person, while sales staff will be hired closer to customers.
Golden also disclosed
pricing
for the first time. The platform has two tiers, a Team plan at $24 per user per month billed annually and a custom-priced Enterprise plan.
Ajenstat said the cost of the underlying AI models is included rather than billed separately, a deliberate break from rivals that pass token costs on to customers. He compared the approach to the steady decline in cloud storage prices, betting that model costs will keep falling.
Early customers include Carta, the equity-management company, whose insights director, Ashley Neville, said in the announcement that Golden gave the firm “the confidence to move on from legacy contracts.”

FAQ
Your questions answered.
How does the trial work?
You get full access to all Enterprise features for 14 days, no credit card required. At the end of your trial, you can choose to continue on a paid plan or cancel.
Is there a free plan?
No. Golden Analytics starts at $24/user/month on the Team plan with an annual subscription ($30/user/month if you do a monthly subscription). You can try the full product free for 14 days — no credit card required.
Do you charge per query, or per AI call?
No. Pricing is per seat. We do not charge per query or per AI call. That's it.
Can I bring my own LLM key?
No. We use a collection of LLM's and models. Golden takes care of optimizing for the specific use cases and capabilities of the models.
Do agents see all my data?
No. We look at the schema of your data, statistics, and a sample of the dataset.  We do not train on your data. We do not send your data to LLMs.
Is Golden Analytics secure? What compliance certifications do you have?
Golden Analytics is currently pursuing SOC 2 certification. In the meantime, we follow industry-standard security practices including encryption in transit and at rest, role-based access controls, and secure infrastructure. Enterprise plans include granular security controls and SSO. Contact us for our full security overview.
What happens when my trial ends?
Your data and work are preserved for 30 days. You'll be prompted to choose a paid plan to continue. If you don't subscribe, your account is paused — nothing is deleted immediately.

Filled Maps for Counties, Provinces and Metro Areas
Filled maps now shade US counties, first-level subdivisions outside the US (Canadian provinces, German Länder and the rest), US metro areas, and the five US island territories, alongside the countries and states they already covered. A county joins on its GEOID or on state plus county name, and a name that can't be identified exactly declines and draws points with a notice naming the remedy.
Distance as a Filter
Type "within 100 miles of Chicago", "within 50km", or "further than 15 miles from a store" into a filter and the chart narrows to it. The center can be a place you name or the rows of another table, so a customer near four stores stays one customer instead of being counted four times. The center and radius stay editable, and an ambiguous place name comes back as a short list to pick from.
Prepare on Live Warehouse Connections
Data prep is now available on live warehouse connections, not just imported files. Steps compile into query-time SQL, so a calculated field or a cleanup step applies to every query without copying the data out of the warehouse.
Charts Across Two Tables at Different Grains
A chart spanning two fact tables at different grains now answers instead of inflating its numbers: each table aggregates on its own and the results line up on the fields they share. You can also declare that two columns mean the same thing, like a real date and a bare month number, and a refused chart opens that dialog itself, pre-filled with a preview of what each column holds.
Geographic Columns Detected From Their Values
A column of ISO codes named `region_cd`, or any FIPS column, is now recognized as geographic even though its name gives nothing away. Detection samples the actual values against ISO codes, country names, US state names, USPS codes and county FIPS, and declines when two geographies fit equally well rather than drawing a confidently wrong map.
Delete Rows by Condition in Prepare Chat
Ask the Prepare chat to drop or keep rows matching a condition, as in "delete every row that doesn't contain investment in Notes". The step replays when the dataset reloads, so the filtered rows stay filtered. Imported datasets only, since warehouse rows are never modified.
Click a Finding to Get Its Chart
Every finding in a published-dashboard answer is now a link, and clicking one builds a chart for that finding on the spot. Previously findings were matched to whichever chart the model happened to produce alongside the prose, so most links went dead a moment after the answer finished streaming.
Date Granularity on the Change Drivers Board
The change drivers board can now switch date grain from its header, not just the measure and the date window. Switching grain re-picks the biggest move at the new grain so the labels stay true, and the board is withheld entirely when there aren't two comparable periods to describe.
Invite People From the Space and Share Pickers
Inviting a teammate no longer means a trip to Settings. An unmatched email in the create-space, share-analysis or member pickers offers Invite to Golden, which creates the invitation and stages the space access in one step. Pending invitees appear in those pickers marked Pending, so an admin can pre-add someone before they accept.
Build a Palette From a Website
Paste a URL into the custom-palette dialog's "Start from" field and the colors come from the page itself. Previously a URL was passed to the model as prose, so it invented colors from the domain name. Picks are intersected with what was actually read, and the dialog says when it fell back to a description instead.
Map Layers
Put a second table's Latitude and Longitude on the Location shelf and its points draw beside the marks in their own color, with a legend naming both.
