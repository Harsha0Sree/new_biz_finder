Warren Raises €10M Seed
Warren, a Ghent-based FinTech startup, has raised 10 million euros in Seed funding to overhaul Belgium's workplace pension system and expand its services into broader European markets. The company previously secured a 3 million euro pre-seed round in early 2025.
Investors
The funding round was led by Motive Ventures. Additional investors included F Capital, Entourage, Syndicate One, and 100IN.
Warren Use of Funds
Warren plans to use the capital injection to refine its technological infrastructure, expand its team by thirty employees, and support its goal of reaching 100,000 users by 2028. The funds will also support the company's strategic expansion into larger European markets.
About Warren
Founded in Belgium, Warren is a FinTech company led by co-founders Cedric De Vleeschauwer (CEO) and David Du Pre (CCO). The company operates the Warren Pension Fund OFP, which utilizes a transparent fee structure to modernize retirement savings, and provides a financial coaching platform that integrates data to offer employees personalized long-term financial planning advice.

Warren

Addressing the Belgian Pension Gap
The company aims to modernize a pension system that
Cedric De Vleeschauwer
, co-founder and CEO of
Warren
, describes as inadequate for long-term prosperity. Current Belgian pension products often suffer from high management fees and poor performance, leaving employees with insufficient savings.
Warren
operates its own pension fund, the
Warren Pension Fund OFP
, which utilizes a transparent cost structure by eliminating entry and exit fees in favor of a fixed subscription model for employers.
Strategic Growth and Future Expansion
Beyond its pension fund activities,
Warren
provides a comprehensive financial coaching platform. This tool integrates data from
Mypension.be
and bank transactions to offer employees personalized advice on long-term financial planning.
David Du Pré
, co-founder and CCO of
Warren
, noted that the firm intends to increase its headcount by thirty employees to support its goal of reaching 100,000 users by 2028.
The startup has already seen significant adoption, with approximately 100 Belgian companies, including
Lighthouse
,
Yuki
,
Wintercircus
, and
Poppy Mobility
, transitioning their pension plans to the platform. By leveraging this funding,
Warren
plans to refine its technological infrastructure before launching into larger European markets, aiming to prove that employer-sponsored retirement schemes can provide tangible returns through disciplined, long-term capital market investment.
