## External Press Release / Source Announcement
Source Title: Exaba secures almost $12M for sovereign cloud storage alternative | Reseller News
Source URL: https://www.reseller.co.nz/article/4183188/exaba-secures-almost-12m-to-for-sovereign-cloud-storage-alternative.html

Looking to help MSPs overcome storage cost pressures.
Credit: 															Shutterstock
New Zealand based data storage startup Exaba has secured nearly $12 million in seed funding to develop sovereign hosted cloud storage solutions.
Exaba chief customer officer and US president AJ Tills told NZ Herald that it would ensure the “country’s data would be stored on New Zealand soil at over 50 per cent below hyperscaler pricing”.
In October last year
Reseller News reported
the vendor would launch a sovereign software-defined storage platform that would offer service providers a “mini-cloud” with the capability to provide an alternative to hyperscalers.
Co-founders Peter Boyle (CEO) and Stuart Inglis (CTO) took their experience from working in the storage industry to address what they saw was a gap in the market: offering object storage to the masses.
“We thought a lot of the solutions out there were potentially focused on enterprise and there wasn’t really a good solution out there for service providers that wanted to create their own cloud,” Boyle said.
The solution from Exaba is an S3-compatible, object storage platform that’s been built for backup, archive and long-term storage workloads.
It’s deployable on off the shelf hardware paired with its own software and offers what the vendor refers to as a “modern alternative” to Amazon Web Services (AWS), Wasabi Technologies and traditional enterprise vendors such as VAST and Pure Storage
“[Service providers are] doing most of the work and they see all of that revenue go out the side door towards the hyperscalers,” Boyle said.
This is because 90 per cent of MSPs store client data on infrastructure they don’t own, while 63 per cent said hidden egress fees are their biggest vendor frustration. As well as two-thirds reported not being able to predict what their own storage will cost next month, according to Exaba’s 2026 MSP Economics Report (US Edition) released in March this year.
“Two-thirds cited hidden fees and unpredictable billing as their single biggest frustration in the industry today,” Tills said his keynote speech at the MSP Summit 2026 in Las Vegas in May.
“They don’t have visibility into what they’re going to be charged, and therefore what their margins are each month. That obviously creates business risk, and it also creates a trust risk with customers.”
“This problem is getting bigger, but that means so is the opportunity. There is significant unmet demand for storage.”
Till said Exaba estimates there are about 120,000 exabytes in the global data sphere today.
“Roughly 20 per cent of that – around 40,000 exabytes – managed by MSPs,” he said. “Data is growing at 23–30 per cent per year over the next decade, while storage capacity is only growing at 15–19 per cent. The gap is widening, and so is the pressure on MSPs.”
Founded in 2024 and now employing more than 30 people, Exaba works with service providers across New Zealand, Australia, and the United States.
According to The University of Waikato the company’s growth has been fuelled by its close connection to University and the HIKO Hub, where Exaba’s headquarters and engineering teams operate.

What security standards does Exaba support?
Exaba is SOC 2 certified, uses AES-256 encryption for data at rest, TLS 1.3 for data in transit, and enforces strict multi-tenant isolation.
What about post-quantum support?
Exaba’s encryption architecture is quantum-ready. As new post-quantum standards evolve, software updates will extend protection seamlessly.
