The company enables both human analysts and specialized AI agents to perform complex threat detection and security posture analysis.

Cybersecurity company Beacon Security has raised $13 million in a Seed funding round led by Notable Capital. The round included participation from Holly Ventures, AlphaDrive Ventures, SVCI, Jefferies Family Office and more than 60 entrepreneurs and cybersecurity executives, including founders of Talon, Descope, Gem Security, Dig Security and Cider Security.

Founded in 2024, Beacon has already achieved rapid market adoption, reporting more than 300% ARR growth in the first half of 2026. The company says its platform is deployed across dozens of enterprises, including organizations in financial services, healthcare, technology and other highly regulated industries.
Beacon was founded by Gal Tal-Hochberg (CEO), Or Mattatia (CPO) and Iddo Israely (CTO), veterans of IDF intelligence units 8200 and 81. Tal-Hochberg previously co-founded HiredScore, which was acquired by Workday for $520 million, and served as Group CTO at Team8. Mattatia previously served as VP Product at Mitiga, where he led initiatives in data and cybersecurity. Israely previously served as VP R&D at Skyline AI, which was acquired by JLL, and led cybersecurity development teams.
Beacon is building what it describes as an AI-native security platform that provides the data foundation and context layer required for modern security operations. The platform connects telemetry from across an organization's security environment, normalizes and enriches the data, and enables both human analysts and AI agents to perform tasks such as threat detection, investigation, detection engineering and security posture analysis.
The company argues that existing security architectures were not designed for an era in which AI is both an attack surface and a tool used by attackers. Beacon’s platform aims to help organizations close this gap by allowing security teams to deploy specialized AI agents while maintaining control over the data and decision-making process.
The platform includes a growing library of Beacon Agents, specialized security agents connected to Beacon’s context layer. The agents were developed with input from cybersecurity experts with backgrounds in offensive security and advanced threat operations. They are designed to identify complex attack patterns, including lateral movement, AI-accelerated attacks and emerging attacker techniques.
"The acceleration of AI agents in the enterprise is creating a distinct need for a legible context layer for cyber defenders, which is fueling a fundamentally new security architecture," said Gal Tal-Hochberg, CEO and Co-Founder of Beacon. "We built Beacon to solve these challenges by providing the trusted data layer that allows organizations to deploy security agents compliantly and effectively. With this investment, we are transforming security for native AI usage to drive real-time posture, detection and response, deep hunting, and operational analysis of security estates at an unprecedented efficiency, scale, and complexity."

The company says its agentic data layer automates the normalization and enrichment of security data, reducing the engineering effort required to prepare information for analysis. Beacon positions this capability as a foundation for allowing security teams to move from traditional tools toward more autonomous security operations.
“Every CISO knows the real bottleneck isn’t detection; it’s trust in your own data. That problem doesn’t go away when you add agents; it gets less forgiving,” said Oren Yunger, Managing Partner, Notable Capital and Beacon board member. “The data-trust gap is exactly what Beacon is bridging in the AI era, bringing context to the security teams and their agentic workforce.”
