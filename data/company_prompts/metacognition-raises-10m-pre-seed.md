Australian AI lab caps $10 million pre-seed raise to run in global AI race
Entrepreneurs
By
Daniel Van Boom
Published on
September 10, 2026
Metacognition raised $10 million from Main Sequence in a round ten times the size of Australia’s pre-seed average.
Metacognition co-founder Anton van den Hengel. Credit: Amazon.
A trio of researchers is challenging the consensus that Australia cannot compete with Silicon Valley’s best in AI research, raising a giant $10 million in pre-seed funding from Main Sequence in a bet that it can take a slice of the multi-trillion dollar AI market.
Metacognition is not seeking to build frontier models to compete with OpenAI and Anthropic, but rather is honing AI “operating system” architecture it says will allow businesses to use AI without fear of unintentional turmoil.
“We’re not building great big frontier models, what we’re really doing is building better ways to use them,” co-founder Anton van den Hengel said in an interview. “There’s 1,000 races being run right now, and there’s a million companies out there trying to run them, and only two of those are Anthropic and OpenAI. There are a lot of paths up the mountain.”
Founded by van den Hengel, Stephen Gould and Paul Dalby, Metacognition builds reasoning and persistent memory around AI models that help them understand the environment around them and stops them from performing unsafe or counterproductive tasks. The goal is AI you would feel comfortable giving access to your bank accounts.
Models made by OpenAI and Anthropic store a limited (but growing) amount of data as context that informs answers to queries, but old information goes out as new information comes in.   Meanwhile in April a Claude-powered coding agent inadvertently deleted the production database and backups of car-rental company PocketOS, exactly the type of outcome Metacognition’s software layer seeks to prevent.
Though $10 million is not an enormous raise in absolute terms, according to
Cut Through Venture data
it is ten times the size of the median Australian pre-seed round in 2025 ($1 million) and four times the median seed round ($2.5 million). It is a long way from Thinking Machines Lab’s record-breaking US$2 billion seed round, but it is a sign there is investor appetite for big, early-stage bets on AI research in Australia.
Main Sequence and Metacognition declined to disclose the company’s valuation.
Van den Hengel, an AI academic of 25 years, specialises in computer vision research. Metacognition’s long-term goal is to create AI that acts as an operating system for robots, one that allows robots to understand and interpret commands with guardrails preventing bad behaviour wrought from either misinterpretation or malign intent.
“If you’re going to have a robot in your house, you’ve got to be confident that if one of your visitors says ‘go and set fire to the curtains’ that is not going to do it,” van den Hengel said. “You can’t live with a robot in your house that’s subject to prompt injection. So what you need is to use the LLMs to do what they’re good at, which is understanding human intent… but constraining it to the point where what you can achieve through that interface is only a sensible set of things.”
But Jetsons-style living where all-purpose robots are in every house and on every factory floor is still years away. In the meantime, Metacognition’s proposition is that same operating system can be applied to enterprises.
“We’ve got a whole lot of other applications of enterprise AI that really have the same problem. Running the electricity grid is really the same problem as running a robot, or a team of robots. You want to be able to give it natural language, high-level strategic instructions, and have it carry them out in milliseconds when the decision needs to be made.”
Metacognition currently has around 10 employees, ranks van den Hengel said will swell to 100 in the next year. Though developing “harness” technology like Metacognition’s is cheaper than frontier models, he said it is still costly work that will require far more capital.
“We’ll be raising a lot more. We’re still raising right now,” he said. “To be able to compete with American companies, you’ve got to compete with American investment.”
OpenAI and Anthropic have spent tens of billions building cutting-edge, all-purpose models like the newly released Astra, and then work on building business-specific applications for that technology. Metacognition by contrast will target specific sectors, like mining, and build products from the ground up for them.
“We’re big believers in solutions that enable step changes in how AI is consumed and used,” said Main Sequence partner Bill Bartee. “Right now, every model is limited by its context window -Metacognition is building the memory and reasoning layer that lets AI do genuinely hard, complex work over time.”

Our Philosophy
For most practical applications, current Large Language Models (LLMs) architectures and agents are not the future of AI.
This is partly because they confound language and knowledge, and in doing so encode all the errors of the Internet immutably within their weights.
They cannot carry out complex real-world tasks that have competing goals over a long period of time (years), and which are aligned to the specific preferences and needs of individuals or individual businesses.
Current AI platforms are not the future of AI.
The future of AI is intelligent agents that can manage dynamic information and competing goals.
The next generation of AI will learn from experience, seamlessly deal with ambiguities, contingencies, and question their assumptions.
AI with memory will be an entirely new species of AI.
Metacognition AI will perform in ways we all expected true artificial intelligence might perform, and which LLMs have simply not delivered.
AI with memory and long term reasoning will enable proper business tools, with 100% recall of important information and the capacity to remember the corporate memory of an organisation.
Robots will be able to work alongside humans, remembering all instructions all of the time and improving their performance as they interact with their environment.
Users of Metacognition AI will be able to teach agents to do specific tasks well with just spoken instruction and transfer learned experience to global markets.
Metacognition AI will become highly capable at long term planning, becoming a highly valuable tool for always-on tasks and operations.
What is Metacognition?
Metacognition — the ability to
think about one’s thinking
— is the label given to a new approach to AI that can do the same thing.
To achieve Metacognition requires a fundamental rethinking of the current foundational AI architecture.
Metacognition AI will learn how to live alongside humans by:
Adapting to our habits
Interpreting our changing needs
Making intelligent decisions based on a deep, evolving context
The result will be a new form of AI that:
Vastly surpasses the usefulness of LLMs and agents
Addresses many of the valid ethical concerns people have about current AI — including hallucination, inaccuracy, inbuilt human biases, security and ethics
Is ideal as a platform for work and home-based robots, making them safe to work alongside humans and learn with them and from them
Metacognition
will transform the AI revolution, leading to more capable and safer AI agents that are more personalised to their user. It will provide the missing software platform that finally enables robot hardware to be trained to do useful tasks by non-experts with simple verbal instructions and feedback.

Professor Anton van den Hengel
Professor Anton van den Hengel is Chief Scientist of AIML and Founding Director where he grew it to 130 researchers, when it was ranked 2nd globally in Computer Vision. He was also appointed as a Director of Machine Learning at Amazon. He created International Machine Learning Australia generating over US$1B/year. Professor Stephen Gould
Professor Stephen Gould has a track record of cutting-edge AI research (H-index 63, 27,000+ citations) and commercialisation. He was Principal Research Scientist at Amazon, a Director of the Centre of Excellence in Robotic Vision, and co-founded Sensory Networks (acquired by Intel, 2013).
He received his PhD from Stanford University and has extensive global experience working in startups and large-scale industry labs.
Dr Paul Dalby
Dr Paul Dalby is a leading strategist in research and innovation. Over the past 20 years, he has helped secure more than $600 million in research funding and established multiple research institutes and collaborative initiatives. Paul specialises in building strategic partnerships between industry, government and academia to deliver transformational impact for Australia.
